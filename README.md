# Scaletru Launchpad

Scaletru — Website Build Prompt (for Lovable)

Attach alongside: Scaletru — Website Architecture & Creative Direction

Build a complete, production-quality website for a digital product studio called SCALETRU.

Do not treat this as a generic agency/portfolio website.

Scaletru is a real business being prepared for launch. The website needs to function as:

The company's primary digital presence.

A sales and conversion tool.

A portfolio and proof-of-capability platform.

A clear explanation of Scaletru's services and process.

A professional demonstration of the quality of digital products Scaletru can design and build.

The website should feel like it was designed by a serious digital product studio, not generated from a generic agency template.

1. Brand

Brand name: SCALETRU Category: Digital Product Studio Core promise: "We Build Digital Products That Scale Your Business"

Primary positioning: Scaletru designs and builds the right digital product for your business — so you can get more customers, save time, and grow without limits.

Scaletru helps businesses improve their digital presence, build digital products, create internal/customer-facing systems, and design better digital experiences.

Scaletru combines:

Business thinking

Product strategy

UI/UX design

Full-stack development

Modern AI-assisted tooling

Important: AI is a leverage tool used internally to improve speed, exploration, development and iteration. Do NOT position Scaletru as an "AI agency." Do NOT make AI the product being sold. The value comes from Scaletru's own knowledge, design ability, development ability, product thinking and execution.

2. Target Audience

Primary audiences:

Small and growing businesses

Startups

Founders

Service businesses

E-commerce businesses

Businesses that need custom digital systems

Founders turning ideas into digital products

Initial market: Nigeria, with potential to expand internationally.

The website should speak to business owners and decision-makers, not primarily to designers or developers. Avoid overly technical language unless it helps explain a product.

Focus on outcomes: get more customers, save time, improve operations, create better customer experiences, turn ideas into useful digital products, build infrastructure that can scale with the business.

3. Design Direction

Overall visual personality: premium, minimal, modern, confident, technical, creative, business-focused, sophisticated, human, intentional.

The site should feel like a combination of: high-end digital product studio + modern technology company + premium design studio.

Do NOT make it feel like: a generic SaaS template, a freelancer portfolio, a crypto website, a gaming website, an AI startup landing page, an overly flashy design agency, or a template marketplace website.

The design should communicate: "We understand business, design and technology, and we know how to bring them together."

4. Color System

Primary background — Near Black #0A0A0A

Secondary dark surface — Deep Charcoal #111111

Primary text — Off White #F5F5F2

Muted text — Muted Gray #A1A1A1

Signature accent — Electric Lime #B7FF3C

Use the electric lime as a controlled accent — for primary CTA buttons, important highlights, small visual accents, selected interface details, hover states, and key moments in the design. Do NOT flood the interface with lime; the dominant visual environment should remain dark, clean and sophisticated. Use subtle borders around cards and sections.

5. Typography

Primary typeface: Manrope. Use a strong typographic hierarchy — large, confident headlines, highly readable body text, heavier weights for hero headlines, section headings, important labels, and CTAs. Avoid excessive use of different font sizes or styles. Typography should be one of the strongest visual elements of the website.

6. Layout

Generous whitespace, a strong grid system, content sitting inside a consistent max-width container. Use large section spacing, clear alignment, strong hierarchy, large visual blocks, full-width product imagery where appropriate, and balanced asymmetry where it improves the design. Do not cram information together — the website should feel spacious.

7. Navigation

Desktop: SCALETRU · Work · Services · Process · About, with a primary CTA — Start a Project →. Sticky navbar, transitioning subtly into a darker/blurred surface on scroll. Not oversized.

Mobile: Logo + simple menu trigger. Mobile menu contains Work, Services, Process, About, Start a Project.

9. Hero

Eyebrow: DIGITAL PRODUCT STUDIO Headline: "We Build Digital Products That Scale Your Business" Supporting text: "Scaletru designs and builds the right digital product for your business — so you can get more customers, save time, and grow without limits." Primary CTA: Start a Project → Secondary CTA: View Our Work →

The hero should be visually powerful. Do NOT use a generic abstract illustration. Instead, create a sophisticated product showcase using Scaletru's actual project interfaces — a composition of polished UI panels/screens from TradeSphere, DirectLet, CRM System, ArenaX, and Emberly. If actual project screenshots/assets are not available, create tasteful abstract product-interface placeholders that look like real Scaletru products. Do not use random stock imagery. The hero should immediately communicate: "We build digital products."

10. Value Strip

Immediately after the hero, a clean horizontal service strip: DIGITAL PRESENCE · DIGITAL PRODUCTS · DIGITAL SYSTEMS · PRODUCT DESIGN. Keep it visually restrained — a quick summary of Scaletru's capabilities.

11. Business Problem Section

Headline: "Your business is growing. Your digital presence should too."

Explain that businesses often outgrow basic websites, manual processes, disconnected tools, and poor customer experiences — then that Scaletru helps turn these gaps into useful digital products and systems.

Three outcome blocks:

Get More Customers — Build digital experiences that help people discover, understand and trust your business.

Save Time — Replace repetitive manual processes with useful digital systems.

Grow Without Limits — Build digital infrastructure that can evolve as the business grows.

Keep this section highly visual and editorial.

12. Services / What We Build

Section headline: "The right digital product for the job."

Four premium service cards:

01 — Digital Presence Websites · Landing Pages · E-commerce "Build a professional digital presence that helps your business attract and convert customers."

02 — Digital Products Web Apps · SaaS · Customer Portals "Turn ideas and business needs into functional digital products people can actually use."

03 — Digital Systems Dashboards · Booking · Ordering · Automation "Make your business easier to operate with systems designed around the way you work."

04 — Product Design UI/UX · Product Strategy · Design Systems "Design digital experiences that are intuitive, useful and built around your users."

CTA: Explore Services →

Cards should have subtle hover interactions — not excessively animated.

13. Work Section

This section is extremely important. Do NOT present every project as if it were a paid client project. Create two visually distinct subsections: A. Client Work and B. Showcase — What We Can Build. This distinction is intentional and should remain clear.

Client Work

Section label: CLIENT WORK

PrintBlink — Website / Digital Presence

Use a larger featured case-study card, structured as:

What was the problem? [Placeholder until actual project details are provided]

What did we build? [Placeholder until actual project details are provided]

What changed? [Placeholder until actual outcome is confirmed]

CTA: View Case Study →

Important: Do NOT invent business results. Keep clearly marked placeholder copy if actual project details are not available. This project should receive the strongest case-study treatment because it represents genuine client work.

Showcase

Section heading: "What We Can Build" Supporting text: "A selection of digital products and systems we've designed and built to explore real-world business problems."

Five project cards:

TradeSphere — Multi-vendor marketplace "A full-stack marketplace built to support multiple vendors, product image management, and live payments — from vendor storefronts to a Nomba-powered checkout flow." View Project → · Visual direction: marketplace UI, vendor storefronts, product management, checkout.

DirectLet — Rental marketplace "DirectLet connects landlords and tenants directly with listings, search and filters, inspection booking, in-app messaging, and payments, built for the Nigerian rental market." View Project → · Visual direction: property listings, filters, property detail screens, booking and messaging.

CRM System — Business operations platform "Built to a full commercial feature spec — leads, sales pipeline, follow-ups, reporting, and notifications. Designed as a real tool for businesses to manage customer relationships." View Project → · Visual direction: dashboard, pipeline, analytics, customer records.

ArenaX — Tournament management platform "Built to run competitive tournaments end to end — brackets, scheduling, and admin score confirmation. Shared directly with the University of Port Harcourt's Director of Sports, whose hands-on feedback shaped features including group-stage standings and team management." View Project →

Important: Do not claim that ArenaX was officially adopted for the university tournament. Position it accurately as a product that received direct stakeholder feedback and feature validation.

Emberly — Habit tracking app "A clean, focused habit tracker built to make daily consistency easier — simple enough to open every day without friction." View Project →

14. Work Card Design

Large, visually impressive project cards, each containing a project visual, name, category, short description, and CTA. Use different compositions rather than making every card identical — the portfolio should feel editorial and premium. Make project visuals large enough to communicate product quality. Subtle hover interactions only (slight image movement, small scale, CTA arrow movement, border/accent transition) — do not over-animate.

15. Why Scaletru

Headline: "More than pixels. More than code." Supporting message: "Great digital products sit at the intersection of business, design and technology. We bring all three together to build solutions that aren't just visually impressive — but actually useful."

Four pillars:

Business Thinking — "We start with the problem and desired outcome."

Design — "We make the experience clear, intuitive and useful."

Development — "We turn the solution into a functional digital product."

AI as Leverage — "We use modern AI tools to accelerate exploration, development and iteration — while our own expertise remains responsible for the decisions and final product."

Important: Do not make the AI pillar visually dominant. AI is an advantage in the process, not Scaletru's identity.

16. Process

Headline: "From idea to impact."

Visual six-step process:

Discover — Understand the business, users and goals.

Define — Determine what needs to be built and why.

Design — Create the structure, UX and visual direction.

Build — Develop the actual product.

Launch — Test, refine and release.

Grow — Maintain, improve and expand the product as the business evolves.

Horizontal timeline on desktop, vertical on mobile. Important: "Grow" connects to Scaletru's ongoing maintenance and growth offering — do not aggressively sell the retainer here.

17. About (Homepage Section)

Headline: "Small team. Serious builds."

"Scaletru is a digital product studio founded by two student-builders combining design, development, strategy and modern technology to help growing businesses build better digital products and systems."

Introduce the founders:

Stephen — UI/UX Designer. Focus: product design, user experience, visual direction and product thinking.

Marvellous — Full-Stack Developer. Focus: development, architecture, integrations and technical implementation.

Supporting positioning: "We may be a small team, but great digital products don't require a massive agency. They require the right thinking, the right skills and the commitment to build things properly."

The founders section should feel human and credible.

Create a dedicated Services page.

Hero: "Digital solutions built around your business."

Expand the four service categories (Digital Presence, Digital Products, Digital Systems, Product Design), each including: what it is, what Scaletru can build, who it's for, what the client gets, relevant project examples, and a CTA.

Do NOT publish internal pricing benchmarks at this stage. Instead use: "Projects are scoped around your needs."

CTA: Start a Project →

19. Process Page

End with: "Need help figuring out what you need?" / "That's okay. We'll figure it out together."

CTA: Start a Project →

20. About Page

Create a dedicated About page including: Scaletru's story, mission, founders, how we work, business + design + technology, AI as leverage, and the small-team advantage. Keep it concise and premium.

22. Footer

SCALETRU — Digital Product Studio "We Build Digital Products That Scale Your Business."

Explore: Work · Services · Process · About Connect: LinkedIn · Instagram · X · Email CTA: Start a Project → Copyright: © 2026 Scaletru

Important: include X. Do not omit it.

23. Responsive Design

Fully responsive across desktop, tablet, and mobile.

Desktop: large typography, spacious layouts, multi-column grids, horizontal process timeline, large project visuals.

Tablet: adjust grid intelligently, preserve hierarchy, reduce spacing where necessary.

Mobile: compact navigation, strong readable hero, stacked layouts, stacked service cards, stacked or horizontally scrollable project cards, vertical process, large tap targets, proper form layout, no horizontal overflow.

Do NOT simply shrink desktop layouts — design the mobile experience intentionally.

24. Interaction & Motion

Use tasteful motion: fade/slide reveals, text reveal, subtle product mockup movement, hover interactions, smooth CTA transitions, card image movement, sticky navigation transition, subtle page transitions.

Principles: fast, smooth, intentional, premium, never distracting.

Avoid: excessive parallax, huge spinning elements, constant floating objects, excessive blur, overly complicated scroll effects, or anything that reduces performance.

25. Image / Visual Direction

Prioritize actual product interfaces — dashboard screens, website mockups, mobile UI, product screenshots, browser frames, device mockups where useful.

Avoid: generic business stock photos, random office photos, people shaking hands, generic abstract 3D shapes, AI-generated people, overused agency imagery.

Scaletru's products should be the visual identity.

26. Component System

Reusable components for: navbar, footer, button, section heading, service card, project card, project showcase, founder card, process step, CTA section, form field, form select, tag/category label. Maintain consistency across the site.

27. Button Style

Primary: electric lime background, dark text (e.g. "Start a Project →") Secondary: transparent/dark surface, light border, light text Hover: subtle movement or background transition

Buttons should feel premium, not oversized.

28. Accessibility

Implement semantic HTML, keyboard navigation, visible focus states, accessible form labels, good contrast, descriptive link/button labels, alt text for meaningful images, and proper heading hierarchy. Do not sacrifice accessibility for aesthetics.

29. Performance

The website should feel fast. Optimize images, animations, fonts, and components; use lazy loading where appropriate. Avoid unnecessary heavy libraries — do not add libraries simply for visual effects.

30. SEO Foundation

Proper page titles, meta descriptions, Open Graph metadata, semantic headings, image alt text, and clean URLs.

Homepage SEO:

Title: Scaletru — Digital Products That Scale Your Business

Description: Scaletru designs and builds digital products, websites, web apps and digital systems that help businesses grow, save time and scale.

31. Important Business Positioning

Never describe Scaletru as: a freelancer, an AI agency, a template shop, or a generic web design agency.

Position Scaletru as: a digital product studio that combines business thinking, design, development and modern technology to build useful digital products for growing businesses.

Tone: confident, clear, professional, smart, approachable — not corporate-heavy, not overly casual.

32. Content Accuracy

Do not invent: client results, revenue numbers, customer numbers, testimonials, awards, partnerships, case study outcomes, client logos, or adoption claims. If information is unavailable, use a clearly marked placeholder rather than making something up.

This is especially important for PrintBlink and ArenaX. ArenaX received direct stakeholder feedback from the University of Port Harcourt's Director of Sports, but should NOT be described as officially deployed for the tournament.

33. Project Detail Pages

Each project CTA should lead to a dedicated project detail page. Reusable project-detail architecture: project name, category, overview, the challenge, the approach, what we built, key features, technology/implementation where relevant, visual gallery, outcome/validation, CTA.

For showcase projects, use "View Project →." For genuine client work, use "View Case Study →." Do not force showcase projects into fake client-case-study narratives.

34. Final Experience

The website should communicate this progression:

"I understand what Scaletru does." → "They build serious digital products." → "They understand business problems." → "They have real work to show." → "They have a clear process." → "They seem easy to work with." → "I should contact them."

The final emotional impression should be: "These guys know what they're doing."

35. Final Quality Bar

Before considering the website complete, review every section and ask:

Does this feel like Scaletru?

Does this look like a serious digital product studio?

Does the website demonstrate our capability rather than just talk about it?

Is the copy clear to a business owner?

Is the visual hierarchy strong?

Are the projects presented honestly?

Is the site fast and responsive?

Is the path to Start a Project obvious?

Does mobile feel intentionally designed?

Does anything feel generic, templated or unnecessary?

If something does not contribute to clarity, credibility, conversion, or the Scaletru brand, remove it.

The final result should feel custom-designed, premium, and production-ready. Do not stop at a basic first-pass template. Build the complete Scaletru website experience.
